# Fresha parity, extra features and user journeys

1. Parity matrix. One row per Fresha feature (walk every page, setting, report and flow in the evidence): Fresha feature and evidence reference; what it does for the business; our equivalent (module, phase.subphase); how ours differs or improves, and why; or "not planned" with the reason. Group by area (calendar, sales, clients, catalogue, team, reports, marketing, online booking, payments, settings, platform). End with coverage numbers per area.
2. Beyond Fresha. Propose additional features that would make this product better for spas and salons in Kuwait and the GCC, and for selling to many companies. Consider for example: Arabic-first experience and Hijri date display, WhatsApp booking and reminders, KNET and local gateways, multi-branch operations (stock transfers, cross-branch staff scheduling, branch comparison dashboards), packages and session bundles common in spas, couples and group bookings, room and equipment scheduling, gender-specific staff or sections, commission and tips rules, client consent and treatment notes, waitlists, deposits and no-show protection, loyalty, gift cards, corporate accounts, a white-label booking site per tenant, a client mobile app, tenant self-serve onboarding and subscription billing, platform admin tooling, audit log, data export. For each: the problem it solves, who uses it, rough size, dependencies, and a proposed phase.subphase. Keep the MVP scope unchanged unless a feature is required for SpaCorner to go live; say so explicitly if you think it is.
3. User journeys. For each role (platform admin, tenant owner, branch manager, receptionist, staff, end client once online booking exists) describe the key journeys step by step, naming the screens and the phase.subphase that delivers each step. Include a Mermaid user-journey or flowchart per role.
4. Residual issues found while doing the above.
